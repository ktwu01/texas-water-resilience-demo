# Data sources

**This repository contains no observational data.** Everything it computes on is
generated by `twr/synth.py`. This file records what a real deployment would need
to fetch, so that replacing the synthetic path is a scoped task rather than a
research project.

Product short names and hosting archives are recorded below because they are
stable and checkable. **Exact URLs, collection versions, and subsetting
parameters are deliberately not hardcoded**: they change, and a plausible-looking
but wrong endpoint in a config file is a trap. Confirm every one against the
archive's own catalogue before wiring a connector. The machine-readable version
of this table is `twr.ingest.PRODUCTS`.

## Observation streams

| Variable | Mission | Product | Archive | Native resolution | Latency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Precipitation | GPM | `GPM_3IMERGHHL` (Late Run, half-hourly), `GPM_3IMERGDL` (daily) | NASA GES DISC | 0.1 deg, 30 min | ~14 h |
| Surface soil moisture | SMAP | `SPL3SMP_E` (L3 enhanced, 9 km) | NASA NSIDC DAAC | 9 km, 1-3 day revisit | ~24-50 h |
| Surface water extent and elevation | SWOT | L2 HR RiverSP, Water Mask Pixel Cloud | NASA PO.DAAC | ~100 m nodes/reaches | days; ~21-day repeat |
| Evapotranspiration | ECOSTRESS | `ECO_L3T_JET` (Collection 2) | NASA LP DAAC | ~70 m, irregular | days |
| Meteorological forcing | NLDAS-3 | NLDAS-3 CONUS forcing | NASA GES DISC | 1 km, hourly | near real time |
| High-resolution reanalysis | CONUS404 | CONUS404 WRF hydroclimate reanalysis | USGS HyTEST / cloud store | 4 km, hourly | retrospective |
| Streamflow | USGS gauges | NWIS instantaneous values, parameter `00060` | USGS Water Services | point, 15 min | ~1 h |

Access to the NASA products requires an Earthdata Login account. USGS Water
Services is open.

## Constraints the table implies

Writing the connectors is the easy part. These are the things that decide whether
the resulting product is operationally honest.

- **SWOT revisit is the binding limit for near-real-time use.** A roughly 21-day
  orbit repeat cannot inform a 7-day capture decision. SWOT belongs in this
  pipeline as *event confirmation and retrospective validation of inundation
  extent*, not as a forecast input. The demo's `water_extent_km2` feature is
  daily, which real SWOT is not. This is the largest single gap between the demo
  and reality, and it should be closed by treating SWOT as an intermittent
  validation stream rather than by pretending the revisit is daily.
- **IMERG Late Run, not Final Run.** Final Run has better skill and a months-long
  latency. Training on Final Run and operating on Late Run is a silent
  train/serve skew. Train on what you will operate on.
- **ECOSTRESS gaps are large and not random.** Cloud cover correlates with the
  rain that matters. Gap-filling must be causal, and a gap-filled ET anomaly on a
  storm day is a weaker signal than it looks.
- **CONUS404 is retrospective only.** It is the high-resolution *training target*
  for downscaling, never a near-real-time input. Using it as a predictor would
  leak.
- **USGS provisional data is revised.** Ratings change after floods, which is
  exactly when the values matter most. A retrospective HMF catalogue rebuilt a
  year later will not match the one built in real time.
- **NLDAS-3 collection status should be confirmed** before it is relied on as an
  operational dependency.

## Basin and infrastructure parameters

`config/basins.yaml` and `config/sites.yaml` are placeholders. Drainage areas,
mean annual precipitation, environmental flow standards, water-right fractions,
and every infrastructure capacity are invented to make the synthetic hydrology
behave plausibly. They carry `provenance: illustrative`.

Gauge identifiers are `SYN-*` and `usgs_site_no` is `null` throughout. This is
deliberate: a plausible-looking but fabricated USGS site number is worse than a
blank, because a blank cannot be mistaken for a citation. A test asserts this
stays true.

Replacing them means sourcing:

- drainage areas and gauge assignments from USGS NWIS and the NHDPlus catchment
  network;
- environmental flow standards from the TCEQ adopted standards for each basin,
  which are seasonal and site-specific rather than a single fraction;
- water availability from the TCEQ Water Availability Models and the water rights
  database, including priority dates;
- infrastructure capacities from utility and district design and permit
  documents;
- aquifer storage capacity and recovery limits from the relevant groundwater
  availability model.

## Partner scales

The three nested scales in `config/sites.yaml` name the Texas Water Development
Board, the Rolling Plains Groundwater Conservation District, and the City of
Kerrville because those are the partners described in the proposal. **No
partner has reviewed, endorsed, or supplied anything in this repository.** The
Rolling Plains district straddles the Brazos and Red basin headwater divide; the
demo attaches it to a single Brazos hydrograph, which a real study would split.
